My Hero Academia is a Japanese superhero manga series written and illustrated by Kōhei Horikoshi. It follows Izuku Midoriya, a boy born without a Quirk in a society where superhuman abilities are common. Despite lacking a Quirk, Midoriya dreams of becoming a professional hero.

After an encounter with the world's greatest hero, All Might, Midoriya inherits the powerful Quirk One For All and is accepted into U.A. High School, an academy dedicated to training the next generation of professional heroes.

The series was serialized in Shueisha's Weekly Shōnen Jump magazine from July 2014 to August 2024. The manga was collected into 42 tankōbon volumes. The story was adapted into an anime television series, along with animated films, original video animations, video games, and other media.

The series explores themes such as heroism, friendship, responsibility, courage, discrimination, society, and the meaning of being a true hero.

Plot

Beginning

In a world where approximately eighty percent of the population possesses superhuman abilities called Quirks, Izuku Midoriya is one of the people born without one. Despite this, he admires professional heroes and dreams of becoming one himself.

Midoriya's life changes when he meets All Might, the world's greatest hero. After witnessing Midoriya's courage during a dangerous situation, All Might decides that Midoriya has the qualities of a true hero.

All Might reveals that his own Quirk, One For All, can be passed from one person to another. He chooses Midoriya as his successor and begins training him so that he can enter U.A. High School.

U.A. High School

Midoriya is accepted into U.A. High School and joins Class 1-A, where he trains alongside other aspiring heroes. His classmates include Katsuki Bakugo, Shoto Todoroki, Ochaco Uraraka, Tenya Iida, and many others.

The students participate in combat exercises, rescue training, examinations, internships, and practical hero activities. Through these experiences, they gradually learn that being a professional hero requires more than simply possessing a powerful Quirk.

Major Conflicts

The students of U.A. become involved in increasingly dangerous conflicts involving villains and criminal organizations. The League of Villains, led by Tomura Shigaraki, becomes one of the major threats to hero society.

As the story progresses, Midoriya and his classmates face increasingly powerful enemies while professional heroes struggle to protect society. The conflict eventually develops into a large-scale battle between heroes and villains.

Final Conflict

The struggle between the heroes and the villains reaches its climax as Izuku Midoriya confronts Tomura Shigaraki and the forces threatening hero society.

Midoriya must use One For All while confronting the history and legacy connected to the Quirk. The final conflict also forces the characters to consider what it truly means to be a hero and how society should treat people who have been pushed to its margins.

Production

Development

My Hero Academia was created by Kōhei Horikoshi and was serialized in Weekly Shōnen Jump beginning in 2014.

Horikoshi developed the series around the idea of a society in which superpowers are normal and professional heroes have become an established part of everyday life.

The series draws inspiration from superhero comics as well as Japanese manga. Its characters possess distinctive Quirks that influence their abilities, personalities, costumes, and fighting styles.

Characters

Izuku Midoriya is the main protagonist of the series. Originally born without a Quirk, he inherits One For All from All Might and enters U.A. High School to pursue his dream of becoming a professional hero.

Katsuki Bakugo is Midoriya's childhood acquaintance and one of his strongest rivals. His Quirk, Explosion, allows him to create powerful explosions using the sweat produced by his palms.

Shoto Todoroki is a student in Class 1-A whose Quirk, Half-Cold Half-Hot, allows him to manipulate both ice and fire.

Ochaco Uraraka is a student who wants to become a professional hero. Her Quirk, Zero Gravity, allows her to remove the effects of gravity from objects she touches.

All Might is the former Symbol of Peace and the previous holder of One For All. He becomes Midoriya's mentor and teacher.

Tomura Shigaraki is one of the major antagonists of the series and the leader of the League of Villains. His Quirk, Decay, allows him to destroy objects and living beings through physical contact.

Setting

My Hero Academia takes place in a world where Quirks have become a normal part of human society. People with powerful abilities can become licensed professional heroes and work to protect civilians from villains.

U.A. High School is one of the most prestigious hero academies. It trains students in combat, rescue operations, teamwork, and the responsibilities associated with being a professional hero.

Hero society is supported by professional agencies, hero rankings, schools, laws, and public expectations. The series also examines the problems created by this system, including discrimination against people with certain Quirks and the pressure placed on heroes.

Conclusion

My Hero Academia presents heroism as more than simply defeating villains. Throughout the story, its characters learn about courage, sacrifice, responsibility, friendship, and the importance of helping others.

Izuku Midoriya's journey from a Quirkless boy who admired heroes to someone capable of inspiring others forms the central story of the series.

The series combines superhero action with school life and coming-of-age themes, creating a world where becoming a hero is both a personal dream and a responsibility toward society.
